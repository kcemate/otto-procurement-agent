A week-long SaaS audit should not end in a spreadsheet nobody trusts.

This is Otto Procurement Agent in ten-out-of-ten mode: a replayable agent-run business, not a static report.

It starts with messy procurement evidence: invoices, usage logs, renewal windows, owners, duplicate apps, and contracts. One click replays the full run: ingest, normalize, challenge, approve, bill, and prepare actions.

The MoA council is adversarial. One model hunts savings. One recomputes the math. One checks provisioning risk. One blocks unsafe cuts. The important proof is the refusal: Otto finds a tempting Slack reduction, then blocks it because of legal hold and executive workspace dependencies.

Every approved dollar has fingerprints. Seven evidence cards tie savings to invoices, SSO usage, owner records, migration maps, and policy controls. The approved actions total sixty-two thousand eight hundred eighty dollars in annual savings.

Then Otto operates like a business. It records a twelve thousand five hundred seventy-six dollar success-fee event, debits thirty-four dollars and twenty cents of operating cost, reinvests five hundred dollars into the next scan, and leaves twelve thousand forty-one dollars and eighty cents in treasury.

It also signs dry-run manifests for the work: cancellations, migrations, seat true-ups, queue routing, and checkout preparation. Nothing dangerous executes without human approval.

This is the point: Otto earns, spends, blocks bad ideas, prepares real operations, and proves the economics of the agent itself.
